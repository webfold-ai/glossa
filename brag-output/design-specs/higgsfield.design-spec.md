# Higgsfield Announcement Motion — Design Spec

Status: reference incumbent spec. Format: RFC 2119 keywords (MUST, MUST NOT,
SHOULD, SHOULD NOT, MAY), written in ASD-STE100 style (short sentences, one
instruction per sentence, active voice, present tense, controlled vocabulary).
Scope: the motion and announcement-structure language of Higgsfield's launch
and funding-announcement content (AI-generated-video-company energy: bold,
confident, proof-driven, community-facing).

## 1. Announcement Structure

1. An announcement MUST open with one bold, concrete claim. The claim MUST
   NOT be vague ("we're excited to share").
2. An announcement MUST follow the claim with concrete proof. Proof MUST be
   a real number, a real artifact, or a real capability — not a vibe.
3. An announcement MUST name who benefits. The named audience MUST be
   specific (a role or a segment), not "everyone."
4. An announcement MAY close with a call to action or a community hook (a
   link, a challenge, an invitation to build).

## 2. Typography

5. A headline MUST be short. A headline SHOULD fit in one breath when read
   aloud.
6. A headline MUST use a heavy or black weight for its hero moment.
7. A number or metric MUST render larger, or bolder, than the copy around
   it.
8. Sentence case or lowercase MAY replace title case for an informal,
   confident register.

## 3. Motion and Timing

9. An entrance MUST be fast. A hero element MUST reach its settled state in
   under 0.5 seconds.
10. An entrance MAY overshoot. A small `back.out`-style overshoot SHOULD read
    as energy, not as sloppiness — overshoot MUST stay small enough to avoid
    a cartoon wobble.
11. A cut between beats MUST be quick. A hold MUST NOT outlast the point it
    makes.
12. A number reveal SHOULD count up or snap in, not fade in passively.
13. A scene MUST commit to one idea. A scene MUST NOT split attention across
    two competing claims.
14. Energy MUST come from pace and confidence, not from added ornament
    (particles, sparkles, lens flares).

## 4. Composition and Pacing

15. A frame MUST feel busier than a calm brand frame, but MUST still resolve
    to one clear read. A frame MUST NOT become illegible clutter.
16. A proof point (a number, a named capability) MUST be the visually
    loudest element in its scene.
17. Pacing across a full announcement SHOULD accelerate slightly toward the
    proof beat, then settle for the closing claim.

## 5. What this style is NOT

18. This style MUST NOT be slow or contemplative throughout — energy MUST
    be present within the first two seconds.
19. This style MUST NOT bury its proof point in supporting copy; the proof
    MUST lead, not trail.
20. This style MUST NOT rely on hedging language ("we think", "hopefully").
